CRITICAL TRANSCRIPT-HANDLING RULE
--------------------------------------------------

First identify the REAL number of candidate interviews.

Do NOT use the number of transcript files as the number of interviews.

Transcript files may be split into multiple files for the same candidate.

For example:

Google-Sohan-1.vtt
Google-Sohan-2.vtt
Google-Sohan-3.vtt

= ONE candidate interview / ONE interview round.

Similarly:

Snorkel AI Goutham -1.vtt
Snorkel AI Goutham -2.vtt

= ONE candidate interview.

Group files primarily using:

Company + Candidate Name

Treat suffixes such as:
-1
-2
-3

as transcript parts or exports unless the supplied data clearly proves they are separate interview rounds.

Before analysis:

1. List all transcript files.
2. Normalize candidate names.
3. Group files by candidate.
4. Merge transcript parts for each candidate.
5. Remove exact duplicate transcript exports.
6. Determine the number of UNIQUE candidate interviews.
7. Use UNIQUE CANDIDATE INTERVIEWS as the denominator for all frequency calculations.

Example:

7 candidates with 18 transcript files
= denominator of 7
NOT denominator of 18.

If RAG appears multiple times across 3 transcript files belonging to one candidate:
count it as 1 candidate interview containing RAG.

--------------------------------------------------
ANALYSIS WORKFLOW
--------------------------------------------------

Analyze the merged candidate interviews.

Do not count casual setup conversation such as:
audio checks,
location,
greetings,
scheduling,
camera setup,
WhatsApp,
connection issues,
or other non-interview conversation.

Focus on actual interviewer questions, follow-ups, probes, scenarios, technical discussions, behavioral probes, and candidate-experience deep dives.

Normalize semantically equivalent questions.

For example:

"Tell me about your current ML project."

"Walk me through the AI system you recently built."

"Explain your latest machine-learning project end to end."

should generally be grouped under a common pattern such as:

"Explain your current AI/ML project end to end."

Do not artificially inflate question frequency because wording differs.
